How to Spot Fake KK8 Sites — Clone Domain Warning

By KK8 Review BD Editorial · Last verified 2026-09-27 · 18+

When you search for KK8 by name in Bangladesh, many of the first sites that come up are not KK8. This page shows which sites use KK8's name or a similar one, what was seen on them, and how to recognise a fake kk8 site for yourself.

The key point

On 2026-09-27, at least two domains were found that present themselves under the name "kk8" and run their own login and registration forms — whatever you enter is submitted to that site's own server, not to KK8. Neither showed KK8's licence number #365/JAZ.

Why it matters

When a site runs its own login form, the username and password you type there go to whoever runs that site. If someone enters their KK8 account details on the wrong site, those details now belong to someone else — and if the same password is used anywhere else, the risk grows.

In the same way, a deposit made on a site like that never goes into a KK8 account. If something goes wrong later, KK8's support won't be able to find the transaction, because it never entered KK8's system.

A checklist to recognise them yourself

Check these seven points every time before you log in or deposit. Once it's a habit, it takes less than ten seconds.

  • Read the address letter by letter. kk8, kkx8, kk88, kk8x — at a glance they look the same. Look at each character separately.
  • Find the licence number in the footer. KK8's official platform shows #365/JAZ. If the number is missing or different, stop.
  • Don't rely on the padlock. HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted — not that the site is genuine. Fake sites have padlocks too.
  • Don't log in through links in messages. Avoid "bonus links" sent on WhatsApp, Telegram or Facebook. Type the address yourself.
  • Bookmark it once you find it. Searching for it every time is the most common way people end up on the wrong site.
  • Apps only from the official source. A third-party APK file carries the same risk — see the APK safety guide.
  • PINs and OTPs are for nobody else. No site, agent or "support" can ask for your bKash, Nagad or Rocket PIN.

Known tactics of fake sites

Beyond the domain, some behaviour should make you suspicious. You'll see it with fakes of any gaming brand, not just KK8:

  • Unbelievable bonus promises. Unusually large "free credit" is often bait to get people to register in a hurry.
  • Pressure to hurry. "Only 2 hours left", "last chance" — the point is to stop you thinking.
  • Being asked to send money to a personal number. If an "agent" tells you to deposit to their own bKash or Nagad number, it isn't the official process.
  • Asking for an OTP before you've logged in. A real platform never asks for your mobile wallet's verification code.
  • An exact copy of the design. Colours, logo and layout that look real prove nothing — copying a design is the easiest thing to do.

Which addresses can be trusted

The simplest way to recognise KK8's official platform is the licence number: #365/JAZ must be in the footer. For players in Bangladesh, KK8's information and official links are gathered together on KK8 Bangladesh official site — the links from there go straight to the official platform.

Remember the two addresses letter by letter: kk8bd.site and this review site, kk8bd.news. kk8bd.net in the list above looks almost the same, but the ending is different — it is neither of these two.

One warning: KK8 hasn't finalised a separate website for Bangladesh yet. So if any site calls itself "KK8's official Bangladesh site" and asks for money or details, check that claim with particular care.

If you've already entered details on the wrong site

  1. Change your password now — on the real platform, not the wrong site.
  2. If you use the same password anywhere else, change those too, especially email and your mobile wallet.
  3. Check your wallet's transaction history, and report any transaction you don't recognise to that provider's helpline.
  4. Tell KK8's support, so they can keep an extra eye on your account.
  5. Report the site. Submitting the address to Google's phishing report form (safebrowsing.google.com) can get other people warned.
